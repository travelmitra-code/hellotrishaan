The Challenge: Understanding PMD

Following extensive diagnostic evaluations across Canada and India, advanced genetic testing confirmed that Trishaan has Pelizaeus-Merzbacher Disease (PMD) caused by a PLP1 gene duplication.

The Medical Analogy

"Imagine the brain as a city full of roads. Messages travel along these roads to control movement, balance, speech, and swallowing. Normally, these roads are covered with a protective coating called myelin that allows messages to travel quickly and clearly. In Trishaan's case, this coating did not develop properly. The roads are there, but the insulation is missing or very thin, causing messages to move slowly or become disrupted."

Because this essential biological insulation is compromised, basic physiological functions become massive hurdles. Trishaan experiences significant delays in motor development, poor balance, low muscle tone, and severe speech challenges, making independent sitting, standing, or walking impossible without aggressive clinical support.

Crucial Development Window

Why Immediate Timing Matters

Trishaan is currently at a critical evolutionary baseline. Early childhood represents the primary window for neuroplasticity—the young brain's unique capability to adapt, reorganize, and forge alternative neural connections.

Specialists emphasize that therapeutic interventions carry their highest impact during these early years when the central nervous system maintains its peak capacity for development. This window of opportunity is open right now, but it will close as he grows.

"This vital window of developmental opportunity is open right now, but it will not remain open forever. As parents, we refuse to look back one day and wonder if we could have done more."

— Trishaan's Family
